The author sets **Who can read this** in the editor. The choices come from your church's configuration; the standard ones are:

- **Only me** — nobody else, whatever else they can reach.
- **Named people** — you, the person the report is about, and the people you add under _Also visible to_.
- **Leadership** — members of the groups your church marked as the leadership audience, plus the person the report is about. If no group is marked, nobody else reads it.
- **Shared** — you, the person the report is about, and the people you add under _Also visible to_.

On the report page, the lock label beside the status opens a panel naming who can reach it.

### What does not give access

- **Rank or oversight.** Campus or cross-ministry oversight does not open a report.
- **Administration.** Administrators get no readership.
- **Being asked.** Asking a leader for attention, an action or an approval does not let them read the report.
- **Confidential.** Ticking it changes how reading is handled, not who may read. See [Confidential reports](topic:reports.confidential).

### A report you may not read

It is not listed, search does not find it, and opening its link says the same as for a report that never existed. Lists may say how many reports are held to an audience you are not part of — a count, never which ones. See [Why something looks like it does not exist](topic:permissions.not-found).

Search is narrower still: an Only me report is found only by its author, and a Named people report only by its author and the people added to it.
