Three separate things decide access, and none of them is having an account.

### 1. Signing in

Signing in proves who you are and nothing more. It grants no ministry, group, leadership position or capability.

### 2. Confirmed assignments

Where you serve is recorded as an **assignment**. You can claim one, but a claim grants nothing until an administrator confirms it, and nobody can confirm their own.

### 3. Roles and capabilities

A role is a bundle of capabilities your church names and configures. There are three capabilities:

- **Campus oversight** — sees one campus's leadership work; does not open confidential reports.
- **Cross-ministry oversight** — sees across ministries.
- **Administration** — administers the installation.

A role's name grants nothing on its own, and neither does what you are called on your person record: "Bishop" is a word the church uses, not a permission.

### What no capability does

**Administration is not readership.** Managing people and structure does not let anyone read pastoral or leadership content they are not an audience for. Seniority does not either. Being asked to look at a record does not make you its audience.
