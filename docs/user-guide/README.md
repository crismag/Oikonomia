## The idea the whole thing is arranged around

Oikonomia asks one question: **what needs my attention?**

That shapes more than the home page. A report published to you is _information_
— it is there to read, and it asks nothing of you unless its author explicitly
said so. Work that genuinely needs you says so, in those words. Nothing is
quietly added to your pile because somebody else finished something.

Two consequences worth knowing on your first day:

**Being asked to look at something is not permission to read it.** Attention
and access are separate. Somebody can draw your attention to a record you still
may not open.

**Signing in proves who you are and nothing more.** Where you serve, what you
may read and what you may change come from assignments your church has
confirmed — not from having an account.

## How the navigation is arranged

The sidebar is a binder, and the groups mean something:

| Group            | What is in it                                                                        |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Home**         | Home, My Progress — where you stand                                                  |
| **My Work**      | Weekly Agenda, Monthly Calendar, Meeting Notes, Reach-Out, Leadership Reports, Goals |
| **Shared**       | LifeGroup, Ministry — work a team does together                                      |
| **Library**      | Documents & Forms, Resource Search                                                   |
| **Oversight**    | Leadership Inbox, Team Overview, Reports to you                                      |
| **Organization** | People, Attendance, Leadership journal, Administration                               |

You will not see all of it. What appears depends on what your church has
confirmed about where you serve. A leader with no confirmed assignments signs
in successfully and sees very little — that is correct, not a fault.

## When something is missing

Oikonomia tries not to show a control that cannot work. If a button you expect
is absent, the usual reasons are:

- **Your church has not configured it.** Google sign-in, and emailed sign-in
  links, only appear where an administrator has set them up.
- **It is not yours to do.** Administration is not readership: administering
  the installation does not make somebody an audience for what is in it.
- **It is not built.** Where that is the case the screen says so plainly
  rather than offering something inert.
