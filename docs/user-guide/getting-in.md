# Getting in

## You do not sign yourself up

Oikonomia is invitation-only. An administrator enters you as a person, records
your email address, and invites you. There is no public registration, and
authenticating with an address nobody has entered does not create an account.

## Your invitation

You receive an email — _"You have been given access to Oikonomia"_ — naming who
gave you access and carrying a link. The link:

- **works once**
- **expires in fifteen minutes**

Following it asks you to choose a password. At least 12 characters; a
passphrase of a few words is easier to remember and harder to guess than
something short and clever.

If the link has expired, ask for a new one from the sign-in screen, or ask your
administrator.

> **If your church has no email set up**, the account is still created and your
> administrator sets your password directly. They will tell you what it is; you
> can change it afterwards.

## Signing in

Your email address and your password.

A failed sign-in says one sentence — _"Those details were not recognised"_ —
whichever half was wrong. That is deliberate: saying "no such account" would
let somebody discover which addresses belong to your church.

**After several failed attempts you are asked to wait a few minutes.** This
applies to any address typed into the box, whether or not it belongs to anyone,
so being throttled reveals nothing either.

### The other ways in, where your church has set them up

**Continue with Google** appears only if your church has connected its own
Google project. Your account is matched on your stable Google identity, never
on your email address — an address can change hands, and matching on one is how
somebody could inherit an account that was never theirs.

**Email me a sign-in link** and **Forgot password?** appear only where a mail
provider is configured. Where it is not, the screen says so and points you at
your administrator, rather than accepting a request nobody will receive.

## Your account and your devices

**Account & security** shows what is true, read from your actual account:

- Which sign-in methods you have, and when each was set
- Every device currently signed in, when it signed in and when it was last used

You can **sign out one device** you do not recognise, or **sign out of all
other devices** while staying signed in where you are. The device you are using
is not offered for signing out — use Sign out for that.

**Changing your password signs out every session, including this one.** If the
reason you are changing it is that somebody else knew it, leaving their session
alive would defeat the change.

## What signing in does not give you

An account is a way in and nothing else. It grants no ministry, no group, no
leadership position and no capability.

Those come from **assignments**, which your church confirms. You can tell
Oikonomia where you serve; that is recorded as a claim and grants nothing until
an administrator confirms it — and you cannot confirm your own. Until then you
are signed in and see very little.
