# The organisation

## People

> _One canonical record per person. Ministries, attendance and Reach-Out
> reference these records rather than keeping their own._

One record per human being, referenced everywhere. The same person is the same
person across a ministry roster, an attendance list and an outreach report —
which is what makes "how is this family doing" a question the binder can
answer.

Searchable by name or role, filterable by campus.

A person's record holds their name, what they are called here, their email
address, their campus and who they report to. **What they are called here is
descriptive and grants nothing** — "Bishop" is a word the church uses, not a
permission.

From a person you can also open the leadership reports they wrote that you may
already read, the Reach-Out reports they wrote or worked on, the meeting notes
they wrote that you may read, the gatherings they lead, and the people who
report to them. Someone else's personal notes never appear there.
Nothing on this page widens access: a report whose audience does not include
you is not listed.

### Somebody who has left

People are **deactivated, not deleted**. They stop being available for new
records while everything that already refers to them keeps working. A report
from three years ago still names the person who wrote it.

Deactivating somebody also stops any session they have, immediately.

## Attendance

> _Marked at the gathering, read here. Only LifeGroup gatherings record
> participation today; services and events do not, so nothing is shown for
> them._

Read that second sentence rather than assuming the page is empty because
nothing has happened. Attendance is recorded at LifeGroup gatherings, and that
is the whole of it at present. Services and events appear in the calendar and
record no participation.

The page says so rather than showing an empty table that implies data is
coming.

## Leadership journal

> _Reflection, goals and development. Private by default; sharing is an
> explicit act, and a derived report never opens the rest._

Where a leader writes for themselves: reflection, development, goals.

Three properties, and each matters:

**Private by default.** An entry is yours until you do something about it.

**Sharing is an explicit act.** Nothing here becomes visible because you were
added to a ministry or somebody gained oversight.

**A derived report never opens the rest.** If a report is produced from journal
material, the reader gets the report. They do not thereby gain access to the
entries behind it. This is the same principle as attention not widening access,
applied to your own writing.

Entries can be deleted, and deleting asks first, because there is no undo.

## Goals

Goals carry updates, a status, a target and evidence.

> **Progress is never a percentage.** "Training for excellence" cannot be 47%
> done. What a goal records is what has happened, what its target is, and what
> shows it — because a number would misrepresent the thing being tracked.

Who may see a goal follows the same access rules as everything else: ownership,
explicit sharing, and the responsibility you carry — with administration
granting no readership.
