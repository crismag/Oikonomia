# Leadership

Three surfaces for leaders who carry responsibility across other people's work.
What you see here depends on what your church has confirmed about your role.

## Leadership Inbox

> _What leaders have asked of you, what is flagged as needing a look, and what
> has come in since you last looked._
>
> **Reports do not become work simply by being submitted.**

That second sentence is the design. Most inboxes turn every arrival into an
obligation; this one distinguishes what somebody actually asked of you from
what merely arrived.

Filter by **All**, **New**, **Attention**, **Actions** and **Approvals**. Each
shows how many are waiting:

|               |                                                                         |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **New**       | Reports that have come in since you last looked, to read                |
| **Attention** | Leaders asked you to take notice, and reports flagged by their category |
| **Actions**   | A leader asked you to do something                                      |
| **Approvals** | A leader asked for your approval                                        |

Something reaches Attention, Actions or Approvals only because its author asked
for it. Nothing arrives there because a report was published, a note was
written, or a task was finished elsewhere. **Flagged by their category** is the
one exception to "asked": reports you may already read whose category is one
your church marked as needing attention.

What you can do from a row depends on what was asked:

- **Attention** — **Open context**, then **Noted**.
- **Action** — **Take it on** (marks it in progress, nothing more), **Put on my
  week** (files it on your Weekly Agenda on the day it is needed, or today),
  **Completed**, or **Can't do it** with a reason.
- **Approval** — **Approve**, **Decline**, or **Ask a question**.

An approval is asked of you for one thing by its author. It is not a review of
the report: there is no reviewer or approver of a Leadership Report, and a
report reaches its audience whether or not anybody approves anything.

**What you are waiting on** lists the asks you made of others that are still
open.

Being asked does not let you read a record you could not already read.

## Team Overview

> _How the leadership team's work and reporting are going, and where attention
> should go._

The page says first whose work you are looking at, and why:

| You are                                    | You see                                | By name? |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | -------- |
| In a leadership group with no campus on it | All leaders                            | Yes      |
| In a leadership group for your campus      | Your campus's leaders                  | Yes      |
| The lead of one or more ministries         | The leaders of those ministries        | Yes      |
| None of these                              | How your campus is doing, in aggregate | No       |

It comes from the groups your church marked as leadership bodies and from who
leads each ministry — never from a rank. Without one of those, the page says
_You can see how the campus is doing, not who is behind_, counts leaders
without naming them, and sends **Look into it** to Reports rather than to a
person.

What is on it, top to bottom:

- **Leaders**, **On track**, **Reporting**, **Need attention** and **Overdue**.
  The last two filter the page when you choose them.
- **Needs attention** — each obligation that is overdue or close to it, with
  **Look into it**.
- **Reporting** — expected, submitted, on time, late, not submitted, and how
  many you have not read.
- **Leaders** — where names are visible, each leader's state across work
  areas; choose one for a quick view.
- **Work areas** and **Reporting over time**.
- **Reporting consistency** — where names are visible, who reported in recent
  periods.

It is not a leaderboard and not a report viewer: that a report is late is
shown; what a report says is not. Seeing that something is behind never opens
the record behind it, and administration is not readership.

## Reports to you

> _Reports that reached you from other leaders, and a way back to your own._

- **Your reports** — your latest Leadership Reports, with a link to all of them.
- **Shared with you** — Leadership Reports other leaders wrote that reached you:
  addressed to you by name, to a group you belong to, or to leadership you are
  part of. Newest first, filterable by **All**, **New** and **Read**, and the
  full list is under Leadership Reports → Shared With Me.
- **Goals** — this year's goals, one person, ministry or group at a time.
- **Work reports** — listed when there are any.

Read state is tracked, so you can tell what you have already seen. **Read state
is not acknowledgement** — marking that you have read something is not the same
as agreeing to it or acting on it, and Oikonomia does not conflate the two.

### What you will not find here

A report whose audience does not include you is not merely hidden from the list
— it is **not discoverable**. Searching for its title finds nothing, and
opening its address directly says the same thing it would say for a report that
never existed.

That is deliberate. Telling you "you may not see this report" would confirm
that a report about somebody exists, which for a pastoral record is most of the
disclosure.

This applies regardless of rank. A private report's author can read it; another
leader, a bishop and an administrator are all refused identically.
